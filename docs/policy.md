# Policy

`Policy` is everything the engine needs beyond the ledger. Required:
`cost_method`, `lot_scope`, `functional_currency`. Everything else has a
default.

| Field | Values | Default | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| `cost_method` | `fifo` \| `lifo` \| `hifo` \| `average` | required | Which lots a disposal consumes: oldest first; newest first; highest absolute unit cost first (ties oldest first); a proportional share of one pooled lot. |
| `lot_scope` | `global` \| `compartment` | required | One lot pool per asset, or one per `(compartment, asset)`. Under `global` a link only classifies a transfer as internal; under `compartment` it also moves lots at carried basis. |
| `functional_currency` | str | required | Reporting currency. Has no lots. Always counts as fiat. |
| `price_time` | `trade_time` \| `daily_close` | `trade_time` | Price at the event's own time, or at 23:59:59.999999 UTC of its day. |
| `quote_path` | list of str | `[]` | Intermediate quotes for non-fiat assets: `["USD"]` prices `BTC` as `BTC/USD × USD/EUR`. |
| `fiat` | list of str | `[]` | Assets priced directly in the functional currency from the `official` source class. |
| `cash` | list of str | `[]` | Assets that fix a trade's value when present on one side. Without it a perp fill would try to price `BTC-PERP`. |
| `trade_valuation` | `given` \| `received` | `received` | Which side fixes a trade's value when neither side holds the functional currency or cash. |
| `fee_treatment` | `expense` \| `capitalize` | `expense` | Below. |
| `position_assets` | list of str | `[]` | Assets that may go net short (perp position assets). Any other asset going negative under its lot key is a `negative_position` exception (the short lot is still opened). |
| `perp_cost_method` | `average` \| `fifo` \| `lifo` \| `hifo` | `average` | Which entries a reduction of a perpetual position on a `notional` compartment closes (below). |
| `opaque_compartments` | list of str | `[]` | Compartments booked as one position by value (policy 05 rule 14; [model](model.md#opaque-positions)). |
| `minor_unit` | int \| null | `null` | Decimal places of the functional currency's minor unit. When set, every money field of the result is rounded to it at output by running total per journal account (each row is the change of its account's exact running total rounded half up, policy 05 rule 20.1), so rows smaller than a minor unit never bias a total; open-lot rows are their accounts' rounded balances; quantities and prices are never rounded, and `proceeds = cost + pnl` holds on every row. |

Strict mode is a run option, not a policy field: `run(..., strict=True)`
raises `PriceGap` on the first missing price; the CLI
flag is `--strict` and returns status 1 when a missing price aborts the run.

## Fee treatment

A fee leg is any leg with `fee: true`; it must be negative.

1. `expense` (default): every fee is an expense `Flow` at market value and, when paid in a non-functional asset, a disposal of that asset at market (with its own realized PnL). It never touches the basis or proceeds of the trade it belongs to.
2. `capitalize`: on an event that has trade legs, the fee legs are valued at market and folded into the non-fixing side of the trade. If that side is received (an acquisition, e.g. buying BTC with EUR or opening a perp with USDC) the fee value is added to the basis of the lots opened. If that side is given (a disposal, e.g. selling BTC for EUR or closing a perp) the fee value is deducted from proceeds, and the `Realized` row reports it in `fees`. No `Flow` is emitted for those fees. The fee legs still leave their own lots (a USDC fee reduces the USDC lots at market). Fees on events without trade legs (transfer fees, gas on a plain transfer, funding) remain expenses.

A fee leg in an opaque compartment is never capitalised: it is an expense that reduces the position's remaining cost, and without a price it stays in the compartment's `performance`. A rebate there (an `income` leg labelled `fee_rebate`) is its mirror: income that raises the remaining cost (policy 05 rule 14.14; [model](model.md#opaque-positions)).

A trade whose fee cannot be priced is left entirely unbooked (`price_gap` plus one `unbooked` per leg), like a trade whose fixing side cannot be priced.

## Liabilities

A `borrow` leg opens a liability worth the market value of what was received; a `repay` leg disposes of the asset given (normal PnL on its lots) and reduces the liability.

The liability is carried at cost until it is repaid (policy 05 rule 9.4; owner, 2026-10-03, decision 24: `liability_valuation` is removed). Repaying realizes the difference between the basis released and the market value repaid as a `Realized` row whose `lots` names the liability id (`quantity` positive like a short cover, `proceeds` negative, `pnl = proceeds - cost`), so borrowing an asset and repaying the same units nets to zero. Its market value before then is portfolio's live layer, never the engine's (rule 39.4).

Accrued unpaid interest is a `borrow` leg labelled `interest`: nothing is received, the liability grows at market value, and the same value is an `interest` expense (rule 9.3.1). A negative one reverses an accrual: the liability releases a proportional share of its basis, which is `interest` income. Interest actually paid is an ordinary `expense` leg labelled `interest` (rule 9.3.2); it does not touch the liability. Interest is never capitalized into the borrowed asset's lots.

## Perpetuals on a settled basis

Policy 05 rule 8: a perpetual's result is booked only when the position is reduced or closed, in the settlement asset; while it is open only its funding and fees are booked. A fill's size leg has tag `position`; it books nothing and changes the **open position** per `(compartment, instrument)`, so positions never net across compartments (`Result.positions`).

1. **`notional` compartments** (Hyperliquid, dYdX, Lighter): the fill's cash leg has tag `notional` and is not booked; it gives the fill price (`-notional / size`) and the settlement asset. `+1 BTC-PERP position`, `-60000 USDC notional`, fee `-30 USDC`. When a fill reduces the position the engine computes realized P&L in the settlement asset under `perp_cost_method` (`average` by default, the venues' method) and books it as `income` or `expense` labelled `realized_pnl`, with `Flow.instrument` naming the position.
2. **`pnl` compartments** (CEX futures, Aster): the size leg carries the venue's fill `price` and `settles_in` (the settlement asset); the position is kept at average entry. The venue's own `realized_pnl` legs are `income` / `expense` legs and are the booked result; the engine realizes nothing itself.
3. **Collateral is untouched by fills**: the settlement asset's lots move only by the realized P&L, funding and fees booked above, so their gains against the reporting currency stay unrealized until spent.
4. **A settlement asset never goes below zero in a perpetual compartment** (a compartment with any `position` leg): an outflow beyond what the compartment holds is owed, a liability `(compartment, asset)` labelled `settlement` at the outflow's market value; a later inflow of that asset into the compartment (income, a trade, a transfer, linked or not) clears it first, realizing the difference between its value and the basis released (rule 8.6). Collateral in other assets is never converted.
5. A fill whose size or cash cannot be read (no notional leg and no price, a notional leg without one size leg of the opposite sign, several size legs against one notional leg) is `unbooked` and the result incomplete, never priced at zero (rule 8.7). So is a fill whose convention or settlement asset differs from its open position's.

Fees on a fill are expenses under either `fee_treatment`: a fill has no `trade` legs to capitalize into.

The legacy booking of fills as served, a `trade` of the position asset against the full notional with the position asset in `position_assets`, still works for callers that send it; policy 05 replaces it (probe 7 shows why: it books FX on the whole notional and disposes of collateral at every fill).

## Why the policy is what it is

Portfolio's accounting policy is a closed set of options, one per portfolio, and a revision is built under exactly one. Nothing outside it is policy: allocations and recognition values are computed from market values, a correction may override one record, and the audit's tolerances are not accounting. Changing the policy needs the `correct` scope: it is an accountant's decision. *Why:* a closed set is what a revision can state in full and what every page can label; the same holding has a different cost under `fifo` and `hifo`, so a figure without its policy invites comparing numbers that are not comparable.

1. **One cost method per portfolio.** Not per account, asset or period; a portfolio that needs two (a tax view and a management view) is two portfolios, or two revisions under two policies. *Why:* tax rules prescribe one method per taxpayer, not per wallet; the engine runs one method per run; and a per-account method would move lots between methods on every internal transfer. `average` pools each lot key into one lot, so acquisition dates are not tracked: a jurisdiction with holding-period rules needs `fifo`.
2. **Perpetuals use their own method, `average` by default** (owner, 2026-09-29). *Why:* it is the method venues settle by, so a book's perp results match what users see on the venue, and every perp in a book, `notional` or `pnl`, uses one method.
3. **Any fiat currency can be the reporting currency** (owner, 2026-09-29). Accounting logic is the same for every currency; what a currency needs is an FX rate for every priced day, so one the price source cannot serve is refused when the policy is set, never discovered as gaps at build time. `minor_unit` defaults to the currency's ISO 4217 minor unit.
4. **Money is rounded by running total per journal account** (2026-10-04). *Why:* rounding each row on its own biased totals wherever rows are smaller than a cent, and the open-lot rows then disagreed with the rows that built them. On the owner's 2025 draft, 4,155 Bitget bitcoin yields of about 0.0047 EUR were each 0.00 of income while their lots kept the cost: yield was 23.89 in the rows against 33.17 exact. On Q3 2026, 113,447 dYdX and Hyperliquid fee rows of about half a cent understated fees by 53.77 EUR. Running totals keep every row within a cent and every total exact to the cent, and keep the engine exact: booking in cents would have moved sub-cent income into later realized gains. Accountants check that a report adds up, so totals are sums of rounded rows.
5. **Quantities are exact, with a 1e-18 net** (owner, 2026-10-03). Quantities and prices are never rounded. A holding a take would leave nonzero but within 1e-18 of zero is treated as zero, and each use raises an `info` item (`quantity_residue`) so new crumbs stay visible. *Why:* at 28 significant digits, taking from lots a rollover had carved left residue lots of about ±1e-28 of the opposite sign, which made a holding look short; on the company books three ETH→aETH supplies (2.15 ETH) were refused as "output requires a non-short holding". 1e-18 is below the smallest unit of any catalogued asset, so the net never swallows a real holding (one wei stays a holding). A crumb is a cause to fix in the unit that served it, not a tolerance to rely on.
6. **Accrual at cut-offs.** Interest that accrues without a transaction (rebasing tokens, debt tokens) is recognised at month ends by default, at year ends, never, or at explicit dates. Portfolio passes the cut-offs to the units, which emit accrual records at them, so changing the cadence changes the inputs and needs a re-sync. *Why:* a balance that grows by itself has no event to book, so the cut-offs create one. A book at a date that is not a cut-off adds the date as one and re-syncs the accruing resources first (owner, 2026-09-30): 10,000 USDC supplied to Aave on 1 January under `monthly`, booked at 15 March without it, holds 10,063 aUSDC and 63 of income while the truth is 10,079 and 79.
7. **A policy change is applied by re-derivation, never by rewriting.** The change is recorded with its actor and its old and new values; derived reads re-derive; every revision made under another policy becomes stale (readable, exportable, replayable, labelled with its policy, never shown as current). *Why:* sealed books are evidence and must not change; a stale mark says that a newer policy exists without destroying what was filed under the old one.
8. **Unsolicited receipts and the dust line** (owner, 2026-10-02). A receipt the unit flagged `unsolicited` (the address did not initiate it) that is not linked follows the portfolio's `unsolicited` option (`ignore` by default, or `income`) only while it is worth less than `unsolicited_dust` (1 in the reporting currency by default), each receipt compared alone at its market value at receipt. At or above the line it is not income: it is booked as an unlinked inbound transfer at market, with a warning, "own transfer, or airdrop?", which the owner answers by linking it, tagging its counterparty, or confirming it as an airdrop. A receipt without a price counts as at or above the line, so a missing price never makes income; a receipt in an asset no catalogue knows is spam and never booked; a bridge payout is never an unsolicited receipt. *Why:* "unsolicited" only means the address did not initiate the transfer, which is also true of every deposit from outside. On the owner's addresses 147 unsolicited receipts split three ways: 53 in tokens and NFTs no catalogue knows (spam, phishing NFTs included); most of the catalogued ones are address poisoning and dust worth fractions of a cent, where income is harmless and asking is noise; and a few are large (9,996.0936 EURC and 1,999.77 axlUSDC were no airdrop, and booking them as income would have overstated taxable income by thousands). A value line separates the two without guessing what a receipt is.
9. **Transfers.** A linked transfer moves lots at carried basis and realizes nothing; the link's residual (never negative) is a `transfer_fee`. An unlinked one is booked by its tag: to or from someone else, a disposal or acquisition at market; to a counterparty marked mine, the lots leave at cost (portfolio's [boundary transfers](https://github.com/litmus-tax/portfolio/blob/main/docs/boundary-transfers.md)). Untagged, it is booked as not retained, with a warning. *Why:* the owner's own funds moving between places are not taxable events; payments are, and only the owner knows which an unlinked transfer is.
10. **A swap bridge is booked as a swap** (owner, 2026-10-02): the asset given is disposed of at market at the withdrawal's time, and the asset received is acquired at that value, with no transfer fee. *Why:* a bridge that swaps on the way (a relayer turning ETH into the USDC it burns) exchanges one asset for another, which is a disposal wherever it happens; carrying a lot across would keep an ETH cost on a USDC holding.
11. **A wrap is a trade.** Wrapping and unwrapping (ETH ↔ WETH) are disposals. A jurisdiction that treats them as non-disposals would get a policy option that carries basis across catalogue-declared equivalents, not a new nature, which keeps the nature vocabulary closed.
12. **Perpetuals are booked on a settled basis** (owner, 2026-09-29). *Why:* notional cash is a venue mechanism, not money the owner holds. Booked as served, it is a short position in the settlement asset: its price moves against the reporting currency realize FX results on the whole notional, offset by an inflated trading result, and the collateral is "sold" at every fill that takes cash below zero. In a EUR book, a 2,000 USDC gain with USDC moving from 0.92 to 0.93 booked as served is +2,460 on the position, −500 on the notional and +200 on the collateral; on a settled basis it is 1,860 realized on the trade plus 300 unrealized on the collateral. The totals agree; the classification does not, and on large notionals the gross lines dwarf the result. Units and verification keep the notional basis; only booking changes.
13. **A negative settlement balance is a liability** (owner, 2026-09-30). *Why:* it is what the venue does: a multi-asset margin account owes the settlement asset against collateral held in another (the owner's Aster account settles in USDT against USDC). Converting collateral to cover it would book a conversion that never happened, and negative lots would break the lot model.
14. **Liabilities are carried at cost** (owner, 2026-10-03). Repaying realizes the difference between the value given up and the cost released, on both sides, so borrowing an asset and repaying the same units nets to zero. Their market value appears only in portfolio's live layer. A liability an event leaves owing nothing releases any carrying value left (2026-10-04). *Why:* the former `liability_valuation: market` option only changed the unrealized side under the split of accounting and valuation, and both values realized the same amount at repayment; remeasuring debt in the books would be a fair-value measurement, which is an open question ([decisions](decisions.md)). Liabilities are identified by their own compartment, so two facilities never merge; accrued interest is always an expense, which is how a borrow of 100, an accrual of 5 and a repayment of 105 shows a 5 expense instead of a silent loss.
15. **An opaque compartment is one position, booked by value** (owner, 2026-10-03). Nobody sees what happens inside a copy-trading wallet, a grid bot or a vault, so the books never hold its contents as coins: what goes in carries its cost into the position, what comes out is a redemption at market value that first recovers the remaining cost, and value beyond it is a `performance` gain. A fee the venue reports inside is still a fee (an expense that lowers the remaining cost); a rebate is its mirror; an unreported or unpriced one stays in `performance`. *Why:* a per-asset residual is a result only while the activity inside never converts one coin into another, and the owner's Bitget grid bots do: bot 3 turned 1,612.59 USDC into 524.64 USDC and 1,087.79 USDT in five minutes, a real result of −0.16 USD, which per asset booked +1,087 of performance in 2025 and −1,036 at the 2026-01-01 snapshot. Across seven bots that was +1,522 EUR in 2025 and about −1,500 in 2026 for a real result of about +11 USD, all in 2025. What can be known about an opaque compartment is the value that went in, the value that came out and the value of its contents at checkpoints; a position by value uses exactly that. The [positions](positions.md) page works the examples; how a measured value (a true-up) reaches the books is still open ([decisions](decisions.md)).
